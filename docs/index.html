Content Refresh & Opportunity Scoring

An ML-Driven Ranking Engine for Prioritizing Content Refresh Opportunities

Author: Youssif Khaled Track: Machine Learning Internship Capstone

1. Title & Abstract

Abstract Identifying decaying content across large client inventories poses a major operational bottleneck for SEO teams facing limited weekly review capacity. This paper addresses the challenge of prioritizing content pages at risk of organic traffic decay using anonymized multi-channel search performance signals. We frame content refresh identification as a machine learning ranking task and evaluate a GroupKFold-validated Random Forest model against a baseline heuristic rule. The trained machine learning model achieves a Precision@50 of ~0.74, delivering a 3× improvement over the heuristic rule baseline (~0.24). The output provides a prioritized review queue with actionable reason codes and refresh playbooks to maximize reviewer efficiency.

2. Introduction & Problem Statement

Content marketing and SEO teams managing inventories of over 30,000 pages lack the manual capacity to audit every URL continuously. Without prioritization systems, decaying pages quietly lose search visibility before intervention occurs.

  • Decision Supported: Allocates reviewer hours efficiently by surfacing a ranked queue of high-demand, declining pages with automated reason codes (e.g., HIGH_DEMAND_DECLINING_POSITION) and recommended actions (REFRESH_CONTENT, EXPAND_CONTENT, MONITOR).
  • Cost of False Positives: Reviewers waste limited operational hours inspecting performing or seasonally fluctuating content.
  • Cost of False Negatives: High-value pages quietly lose rankings and revenue without notice.

3. Data & Scope

The study uses the FlyRank anonymized warehouse dataset (content_refresh_anonymized.csv) covering 30,000 rows across 32 pseudonymous clients.

  • Unit of Analysis: One row = One unique content item (content_id) for a client (client_id) over a 90-day snapshot window.
  • Observation Window (T - 90d to T): Historical features including impressions_90d, clicks_90d, avg_position, pageviews_90d, and engaged_sessions_90d.
  • Outcome Window (T to T + 30d): Target variable target derived from trend_direction == 'down'.
  • Public-Safe Isolation: Raw URLs, domains, customer queries, and exact internal metadata are strictly excluded.

4. Methodology

To prevent data leakage, we enforce strict group boundaries and split data based on client entities:

  • Validation Strategy: 5-fold GroupKFold split grouped by client_id to ensure client content never leaks between training and testing sets.
  • Rule Baseline: A heuristic formula combining logarithmic impression volume and average position inverse:
log10(impressions_90d + 1) × (1 + 1 / avg_position)
  • ML Model: A Random Forest Classifier trained exclusively on non-leaked 90-day observation features.
  • Leakage Safeguard: Direct outcome columns (trend_direction and trend_pct) are strictly isolated as target definitions and excluded from input features (X).

6. Limitations & Honest Framing

  • Observational Signal: The model identifies historical association and directional risk, not causal proof that updating a page will guarantee ranking recovery.
  • No Search Engine Claims: Results reflect empirical correlations within the dataset and do not represent internal Google ranking factors.
  • External Unobservables: The model cannot account for unmeasured real-time core algorithm updates or competitor off-page backlink changes outside the 90-day window.

7. Ranked Recommendations (Action Playbook)
